People expect a VIN to spill everything about a car: the color, the trim, the options, the whole history. It does not quite work that way. A VIN reliably tells you who built the car, where, and when, plus a coded description of the vehicle type. The rest depends on the manufacturer's own records. Here is exactly what you can pull from the number, and where it stops.

## What Every VIN Tells You for Certain

Four facts come straight out of any valid 17-character VIN, no database needed:

- **Country of origin.** The first character marks the region and country of the plant. A 1, 4, or 5 means the United States. J is Japan, W is Germany, K is South Korea.
- **Manufacturer.** The first three characters together, the World Manufacturer Identifier, name the maker. 1HG is Honda in the US. WBA is BMW.
- **Model year.** The tenth character codes the year, on a cycle that repeats every 30 years.
- **Assembly plant.** The eleventh character points to the exact factory that built the car.

These are baked into the standard, so a decoder reads them the same way every time. Drop a number into our [VIN Decoder](/vin-decoder/) and those four show up in a second.

## The Part Only the Maker Fully Controls

Characters four through eight are the Vehicle Descriptor Section. This stretch describes the model, body style, engine, and safety equipment. The tricky part: every automaker codes it their own way. There is no single public table that turns "GBH" into "four-door sedan with a 2.0-liter engine" across all brands.

A good decoder still reads a lot of it, because the big makers follow published patterns. But two cars from different brands can share the same descriptor characters and mean completely different things. That is why a VIN check gives you the maker and year with total confidence, and the exact trim with a bit less.

## Where the VIN Runs Out

A VIN is a fingerprint, not a full spec sheet. On its own, it does not tell you:

- The paint color or interior
- The option packages that were fitted
- The mileage or number of owners
- Accident or title history

That information lives in the manufacturer's build records and in vehicle history services, which look up the VIN in their own databases. The number is the key. The database is the door. For anything about a car's past, you pair the VIN with an official history report.
