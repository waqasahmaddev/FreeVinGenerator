Buy a used car in the Philippines and the chassis number comes up fast. It is on the Certificate of Registration, the seller mentions it, and any serious check starts with it. But you are standing next to the car and have no idea where to look, or what to compare it with.

Here is how to find the chassis number, what it is, and how to check it before any money changes hands.

## Is the Chassis Number the Same as the VIN?

For most cars, yes. The chassis number is the vehicle's main identifier, and on cars from the big brands it is the 17-character VIN. In everyday Philippine use people say "chassis number" and mean exactly that.

There is one wrinkle. Some locally built vehicles, jeepneys being the common example, carry a chassis number but not a full 17-character VIN. So a shorter number is not automatically a red flag on one of those. For a car from a major maker, you should expect 17 characters. Our reference on [what a VIN is called around the world](/vin-names-around-the-world/) shows how other countries handle the same split.

## Chassis Number vs Engine Number

Your Certificate of Registration lists both, and they are different things. The chassis number identifies the whole vehicle and stays with it. The engine number identifies only the engine and can change if the engine is swapped. Our guide on [VIN vs engine number](/blog/vin-vs-engine-number/) explains the difference in detail.

When you check a car, check both. An engine number that does not match the papers is a common sign of a swap, and a swap you were not told about is worth asking about.

## Where to Find the Chassis Number on the Car

| Location | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Certificate of Registration | Listed alongside the engine number. |
| Base of the windscreen | A small plate you can read through the glass from outside. |
| Door frame | A label or plate on the door pillar. |
| Engine bay or frame | Stamped into the metal, often on the bulkhead or frame. |

The stamped number matters most, because a printed label is easier to fake than metal. The number should be clear and readable. Our full list of spots is in [where to find your VIN](/blog/where-to-find-your-vin/).

## Match the Car to the Papers

Do this in person, with the Certificate of Registration in your hand.

1. Read the stamped chassis number on the car, character by character.
2. Compare it to the Certificate of Registration. Every letter and digit should match.
3. Do the same for the engine number.
4. Look at the stamping itself. Grinding, filler, welds or fresh paint around the number should make you stop and ask questions.

If the numbers do not line up, do not argue it out in the driveway. Walk away until the seller can explain it in writing. Our guide to [checking if a car is stolen by VIN](/blog/how-to-check-if-a-car-is-stolen-by-vin/) covers what a tampered number can mean.

## Read the Number Itself

A genuine 17-character number tells you a lot before you check anything else. The first characters give the country and maker, and the tenth gives the model year. Paste it into our [VIN Decoder](/vin-decoder/) to read them, and see [how to read a VIN](/blog/how-to-read-a-vin/) for what each position means.

The decoder and validator follow the North American standard, so a number from an Asian or European plant may decode for country and maker but fail the check-digit test. That does not mean it is fake. We explain why in [what a VIN check digit is](/blog/what-is-a-vin-check-digit/).

## Checking Against LTO Records

Our tools read the structure of the number. They do not hold registration records, and they cannot tell you who owns the car or whether there is an outstanding issue. For that, the Land Transportation Office runs its own online services for checking a vehicle's registration status. Rules and portals change, so use the LTO's official site for the current process rather than a third-party page, and be careful about sites that charge for what the LTO provides.

## What to Do Before You Pay

- Match the stamped chassis and engine numbers to the Certificate of Registration.
- Make sure the document is the original, not a copy you cannot verify.
- Check the registration status with the LTO before you hand over a deposit.
- Get the seller's name to match the registered owner.
