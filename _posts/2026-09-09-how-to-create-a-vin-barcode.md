Look at the sticker inside a car's driver door and you will see the VIN printed twice: once as text, and once as a barcode. That barcode is how shops, dealers, and DMV systems scan a VIN instead of typing 17 characters by hand. You can make the same kind of barcode for any VIN in a few seconds, for free. Here is how a VIN barcode works, which format to use, and how to create one.

## What a VIN Barcode Is

A VIN barcode is a scannable barcode that stores a Vehicle Identification Number. Point a barcode scanner at it and the full 17-character VIN appears, with no typing and no transposed characters.

The standard format is **Code 39**, a linear (one-dimensional) barcode made of tall vertical bars. Code 39 handles the letters and numbers a VIN uses, and it is exactly what the factory prints on the door-jamb label. That is why scanners and many dealer and DMV systems expect Code 39 when they read a VIN.

## Barcode or QR Code?

Both store a VIN, but they are built for different jobs.

| Feature | Code 39 barcode | QR code |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Row of vertical bars | Square grid of dots |
| Read by | Laser barcode scanner | Any phone camera |
| Matches | Factory door-jamb label | Apps and custom tags |
| Damage tolerance | Lower | Higher |

If you need the traditional format that matches factory labels and works with a scanner, make a barcode. If you want something a phone camera reads easily, make a QR code with our [VIN QR Code Generator](/vin-qr-code-generator/). Many shops use both.

## How to Scan a VIN Barcode

Code 39 is read by a standard laser or CCD barcode scanner, the same kind used at parts counters and service desks. Many inventory and shop-management apps also scan Code 39 through a phone camera. When scanned, the barcode returns the VIN as plain text, ready to drop into your system.

If your team only has phones and no scanner, a QR code may be the easier choice, since every phone camera reads QR natively. This is the main practical reason to pick one format over the other.

## Where VIN Barcodes Are Used

A scannable VIN barcode saves time anywhere a VIN gets entered by hand:

- **Repair shops.** Attach the barcode to the work order so the VIN travels with the job.
- **Dealership inventory.** Tag each vehicle and scan it into the system.
- **Parts departments.** Link a part or a file to the right vehicle by scan.
- **Fleet management.** Check vehicles in and out without manual entry.
- **Auctions.** Identify high volumes of vehicles quickly and reliably.

In every case the point is the same: fewer keystrokes, fewer errors. One wrong character sends a record to the wrong vehicle, and a scan removes that risk.

## Tips for a Barcode That Scans Every Time

- **Test it once** before printing a batch.
- **Keep the bars sharp** by printing at high resolution.
- **Use black bars on white** for the best contrast.
- **Leave clear space** on both ends of the barcode so the scanner can find it.
- **Keep the VIN text** under the bars as a human-readable backup.
