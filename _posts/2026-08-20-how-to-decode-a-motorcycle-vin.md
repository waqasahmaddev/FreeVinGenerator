A motorcycle VIN works the same way a car VIN does. Seventeen characters, the same ISO 3779 standard, the same check digit. What changes is where you find it and which manufacturer codes show up. If you can read a car VIN, you can read a bike VIN in about a minute. Here is how.

## A Bike VIN Uses the Same 17-Character Structure

Every motorcycle built for road use since 1981 carries a 17-character VIN split into the same three sections as a car:

- **Positions 1-3:** the World Manufacturer Identifier (WMI), which names the maker and country.
- **Positions 4-8:** the model, engine size, and type.
- **Position 9:** the check digit that keeps the number honest.
- **Positions 10-17:** the model year, plant, and serial number.

So the pattern from our guide on [how to read a VIN](/blog/how-to-read-a-vin/) applies directly. The only trick is knowing the motorcycle-specific manufacturer codes.

## Where to Find the VIN on a Motorcycle

Bikes hide the VIN in different spots than cars. Check these first:

- **The steering neck (head tube)** - the frame section just below the handlebars, where the front forks meet the frame. This is the most common spot.
- **The frame near the bottom of the front forks** or along the lower frame rail.
- **A stamped plate or sticker** on the frame, sometimes near the engine.

One thing that trips people up: the number stamped on the **engine case is not the VIN**. It is a separate engine number. The VIN lives on the frame, because the frame is the vehicle. Swap the engine and the VIN stays the same.

## Common Motorcycle Manufacturer Codes

The first three characters tell you who built the bike. Here are the codes you will run into most:

| WMI | Manufacturer |
|-----|--------------|
| 1HD, 5HD | Harley-Davidson (USA) |
| JH2, 1HF | Honda motorcycle (Japan / USA) |
| JYA, 9C6 | Yamaha |
| JKA, JKB | Kawasaki |
| JS1 | Suzuki |
| ZDM | Ducati |
| WB1, WB4 | BMW Motorrad |
| SMT | Triumph |
| VBK | KTM |
| MEG | Royal Enfield |

## Reading the Rest of the Number

After the WMI, the middle characters (positions 4-8) describe the specific model, engine displacement, and body type. Each maker codes this section its own way, so there is no single lookup table. Position 10 gives the model year, and our [VIN model year codes chart](/blog/vin-model-year-codes-chart/) decodes it for any year.

## A Note on Older and Small Bikes

Not every two-wheeler has a 17-character VIN. Motorcycles built before 1981 often used shorter, non-standard frame numbers. Some very small scooters, mopeds, and off-road-only bikes may also carry a shorter identifier instead of a full VIN. If your number is not exactly 17 characters, it predates the standard or the machine was never registered for road use.

## Put It to Use

Decoding a bike VIN is worth the minute it takes. It confirms the maker and year before you buy, helps you order the right parts, and lets you check the number against the frame to catch tampering.
