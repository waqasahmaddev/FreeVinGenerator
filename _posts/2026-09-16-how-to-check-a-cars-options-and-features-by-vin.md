You are looking at a used car and the listing says "loaded." What does that actually mean? Does it have the tow package, the sunroof, the upgraded stereo? The VIN can answer a lot of this, and where the VIN runs out, it points you to the document that has the rest. Here is how to check a car's options and features by VIN, and where the real limits are.

## What the VIN Tells You on Its Own

The 17 characters of a VIN carry a description of the vehicle in positions four through eight, the part called the VDS. That block encodes things like the model line, body style, restraint system, and engine. It does not spell out every option, but it pins down the version of the car you are looking at.

That already answers the big questions: which trim, which engine, which body style. ## Trim Level Does a Lot of the Work

Here is a shortcut most people miss: once you know the exact trim, you know most of the standard features. Trims are bundles. An EX-L or a Limited or a Platinum comes with a defined set of equipment, so confirming the trim from the VIN tells you what the car includes by default.

| If the VIN confirms... | You can assume the trim's standard kit, such as... |
|---|---|
| A top trim (Limited, Platinum) | Leather, premium audio, driver aids |
| A mid trim (EX, SEL) | Alloys, upgraded infotainment, extra safety features |
| A base trim (LX, S) | The core equipment, fewer extras |

Confirm the trim, look up that trim's feature list for the model year, and you have most of the answer without any extra lookup.

## Where the VIN Runs Out: The Build Sheet

What the VIN does not list is every individual factory option a specific car was ordered with, the standalone extras on top of the trim. For that, you need the build sheet or the window sticker, which are keyed to the VIN but held by the manufacturer.

- **The window sticker (Monroney label)** lists every factory option with its price. See [how to read a car's window sticker and find it by VIN](/blog/how-to-read-a-window-sticker-and-find-it-by-vin/).
- **The build sheet** is the factory's internal spec, often available from a franchised dealer or the maker by VIN.
- **Option codes** appear on a label in the glovebox, trunk, or door jamb on many cars. These short codes map to specific options, though decoding them is brand-specific.

So the practical split is simple: the VIN and its decoder give you the trim and the mechanicals, and the window sticker or build sheet gives you the exact option list.

## Confirm Against the Car

The VIN and the paperwork tell you what the car left the factory with. They do not know what happened since. An owner may have added aftermarket wheels or removed a factory part. So use the VIN to build the expected feature list, then verify it against the actual car. Anything that does not match is worth a question before you buy.
